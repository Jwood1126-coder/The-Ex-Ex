# Gap Analysis — The Research Corpus Audited (2026-07-07)

> **What this is.** A professional audit of the project's research: what exists, what's
> missing for a well-rounded story, ranked by (story value × feasibility), with source
> paths and owners. Re-run this audit whenever a major tranche lands; strike gaps as they
> close.
> **Method.** Coverage checked three ways: by witness (whose eyes we have), by event (which
> beats have primary support), and by story-function (engine, frame, ledger, texture,
> shore). Then the corpus audited against itself (weaknesses of what we've built).

## 2. The gaps, ranked

### GAP 1 ★★★ — External eyes: the port-town press. *(feasible now; unmined; high yield)*
The squadron was *watched* everywhere it went, and we hold none of it. Confirmed available:
- **The Polynesian (Honolulu), June 1840–Dec 1841, digitized and searchable free at
  Chronicling America** — its run exactly brackets the squadron's Honolulu residence, the
  re-enlistment crisis, and the Mauna Loa campaign. A town's-eye view of the Ex. Ex. at
  its moral low point: arrivals, notices, prices, sermons, possibly the floggings' echo.
- **Sydney press via Trove** (Herald etc., digitized free) — the night entrance "the
  newspapers took the point at once" [E-base] is *checkable and quotable*; also the
  "wills made" climate and the Peacock's battered February return.
- **New York papers, June 1842** — test the base's "came home to nothing" against the
  actual columns (Herald, Tribune; digitized variously).
- **Niles' National Register + Army & Navy Chronicle** (digitized, PD) — the decade of
  "Deplorable Expedition" mockery, the departure, and above all **the courts-martial
  coverage** (see GAP 4).
**Why it matters:** the book's outside-in beats (Sydney's awe, the homecoming's silence,
the trial's publicity) currently rest on synthesis. Period press converts them to [E] with
quotable period voice — and it's the only class of witness with no stake in the squadron's
quarrels. **Owner: model sessions (all PD, all fetchable).**

### GAP 2 ★★★ — The 1890 frame has zero research behind it. *(feasibility high; nothing exists)*
If the frame carries the novel (F0–F2 all use it), we need: old Erskine's Boston/environs
in 1890; the panorama-lecture circuit's mechanics (how a flogged sailor became a paying
act); veteran memory culture of the 1880s–90s (the age that made memoirs sellable); the
1896 publisher (George W. Jacobs & Co., Philadelphia) and how such books were produced and
sold; what Erskine's world knew of Wilkes at his 1877 death; TYBTM's own front/back matter
(prefaces, dedications, subscription lists?) — **which we already hold in the local full
text and haven't harvested** [start there]; any trace of Erskine's family, poverty or
comfort, and death [genealogy-lite; may dead-end]. **Why:** the confession's frame is
currently pure conception with no period floor under the old man's feet. **Owner: model
(text + web); Jake for any paid genealogy.**

### GAP 3 ★★★ — The pin scene's stage: the *Vincennes*' actual deck geography. *(now load-bearing)*
The F6 correction (deck level, lee quarter, cabin skylight, Wilkes at a table below) makes
ship-geometry the crown jewel's staging problem: where the cabin skylight sat relative to
the wheel, the mizzen fife-rail's pin racks, the officer of the deck's walk, sightlines at
night. Sources: the *Vincennes*' Boston-class sloop plans (Chapelle's *American Sailing
Navy* for facts; any digitized draughts); Wilkes' appendices (internal arrangements,
routine orders — **held locally, unharvested**); period sloop-of-war conventions [W].
Deliverable: a one-page deck-geography brief in `research/` that the prologue and every
deck scene can trust. **Owner: model; [VERIFY] discipline heavy.**

### GAP 4 ★★ — The trial record. *(Act V's spine; currently one paragraph of synthesis)*
Charges, specifications, testimony highlights, the reciprocal prosecutions' choreography,
Guillou's evidence, the journals-as-exhibits mechanics [flagged since pass 2]. Paths:
Niles' Register (GAP 1 overlap), period naval-court publications, Wilkes' published
defense; congressional serial set where digitized. **Owner: model first (press layer),
Jake second (any purchased microfilm/ILL if the press layer runs dry).**

### GAP 5 ★★ — The encountered world's own witnesses. *(dignity-critical; partly feasible)*
We hold zero non-American eyes. Available seams:
- **David Cargill** — the Methodist missionary *at Rewa during the Vendovi arrest*; he
  appears in Wilkes' own chapter heads ("VISIT FROM MR. CARGILL"). His 1841 memoir volume
  is public domain; his full diaries (1977 edition) are copyright but fact-mineable via
  scholarship. A contemporaneous third-party witness inside the Vendovi story.
- **Fijian oral-historical scholarship on Malolo** [OPEN since pass 3] — the archive test's
  most wanted item.
- **Hawaiian-language press and mission records** (Lahainaluna's papers predate The
  Polynesian) — likely thin on the squadron, worth one sweep.
- **Hale's *Ethnography and Philology* (1846, PD)** — the expedition's own most
  dignity-compatible volume: languages, migrations, names rendered carefully; also the
  source for getting Pacific terms *right* in the novel.
**Owner: model (PD layers), with findings routed through the witness-class rules.**

### GAP 6 ★★ — The shore half of the book: the women. *(structural if F2 wins)*

## 3. Corpus self-audit (weaknesses of what we've built)

1. **The texture trilogy is synthesis with an unpaid [VERIFY] debt** (~30 tags). It's
   tagged honestly, but until the web-verification pass runs, it's a scaffold, not a
   floor. *(Mitigation: GAP 9 includes the queue; nothing untagged has entered the base.)*
2. **OCR quotes are not yet scan-verified** — every extract carries the caveat; no `fact:`
   commit should move until its page is eyeballed (Jake or model via Wikisource images).
3. **Single-witness settlements.** Several "settled" items rest on one witness (Erskine's
   pin geometry; Wilkes' orders as his own defense). Where witnesses *can* be doubled
   (Clark for below-decks, press for public events), double them before the base moves.
4. **American tilt.** Eight files of American eyes and [W]-context against zero
   encountered-world witnesses (GAP 5) — currently the corpus reproduces the archive's own
   asymmetry. The archive test demands we at least document the silence source by source.
5. **Joy deficit.** The corpus indexes suffering better than delight; the story needs both
   (the base's own wonder register). The iceberg play, crossing-the-line, port joys are
   flagged but unharvested — keep them priority-equal with the dark beats.
