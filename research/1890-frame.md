## Part I — The book as object (all [E], 1890 edition)

- **Title page:** "TWENTY YEARS BEFORE THE MAST / With the more thrilling scenes and
  incidents while circumnavigating the globe under the command of the late Admiral
  Charles Wilkes / 1838–1842 / CHARLES ERSKINE / With Numerous Illustrations / BOSTON /
  Published by the author / 1890." (OCR 65–87)
- **Cover illustrations, chosen for his own binding:** Sailor's Neckerchief, Boatswain's
  Colt, Friendship Knot — **the instrument he was flogged with, stamped on the cover of
  his own book.** (OCR 35–63)
- **Dedication leaf** (OCR 119–128): "To THE CREW OF THE / **Ship Universal** / THIS BOOK
  IS / RESPECTFULLY DEDICATED / BY ONE OF THEIR SHIPMATES / THE AUTHOR" — "Ship
  Universal" in ornamental/blackletter type. ⚑ typography from OCR line breaks; check
  the leaf image.
- **Epigraph leaf** (OCR 141–143): "*Every man is a valuable member of society who, by
  his observations, researches, and experience, procures knowledge for men.* — Smithson."
- **Frontispiece pair — the frame in miniature:** "CHARLIE ERSKINE, Late Coxswain of the
  United States Brig *Porpoise*. From a Daguerreotype taken by Plumb, 75 Court St.,
  Boston, in 1842" AND "MR. CHARLES ERSKINE, The Author. Photograph taken by J. W.
  Black, 333 Washington St., Boston, 1882." Forty years between sittings. (OCR 35–63,
  277–408)
- **Preface "To the Reader," pp. v–viii, signed by Erskine himself** (no editor, no
  second-party introduction anywhere): "only a common sailor before the mast," "no
  college or even common school education," "The world has been my school"; the
  hero-catalog (Columbus, Cook, Wilkes, D'Urville, Franklin, De Long); and the method
  disclosure — "I have refreshed my memory by reading the history of our cruise… under
  the command of the late Admiral Charles Wilkes… indebtedness to the same for dates and
  a few facts and illustrations." **The memoir openly leans on Wilkes' Narrative for its
  dates — custody-theme fact.** (OCR 146–274)
- **No back matter** in this copy: no appendix, subscription list, or ads; ends with the
  Conclusion (pp. 309–311) and a tail-piece. ⚑ 1896 printing may differ.
- **Plates include** "Massacre of Underwood and Wilkes Henry" (facing p. 174) — the
  old man chose to illustrate Malolo.

## Part II — The writing-present (asides from the 1890s, all [E])

- **The confession's own frame, pp. 19–20:** "I am now about to reveal a secret that has
  been smothered in my breast for fifty long years. Would to God I could blot it from my
  memory! Through all these years it has been known to none save myself and to Him whose
  all-seeing eye is ever upon us." And after: "I felt as if I had committed the act, and
  were a criminal in thought, if not in deed."
- **Sole-survivor belief, pp. 121–122:** "I am the only known survivor of the six
  hundred and eighty-seven men who served in the expedition under Commodore Wilkes…
  but my American pride will not allow me to [keep silent]." **His claim — demonstrably
  shaky (Reynolds d. 1879, but other veterans likely lived); use as the old man's
  belief, never as fact.** Also: English maps of "to-day" omitting the American
  discovery — the priority wound alive in 1890.
- **London, "a few months since"** (p. 196): Salvation Army barracks at Camberwell,
  Colonel Barker on Maori converts — the old sailor in the empire's capital months
  before finishing the book.
- **Fiji fifty years on (pp. 195–197):** "perhaps I may be pardoned for thinking it
  would have been better if the islands had never been discovered by Europeans; not that
  Christianity is a failure, but that our civilization is. Nations are like individuals
  — selfish, selfish, selfish." Quotes Dr. T. P. Lucas ("Cries from Fiji") on the labor
  traffic as "a veritable British slave-trade." **The memoir's own double ledger.**
- **Flogging-reform polemic (pp. 264–265):** "Fifty years have passed, and how little
  reform has been made in the treatment of sailors!… My God! is this our civilization?"
- **Relics in his possession as he writes:** four of the six vases President Monroe sent
  his mother (p. 2); a fragment of Napoleon's tomb cement from St. Helena (p. 262); "our
  collection at the Smithsonian Institute" — his phrase for the expedition's seals
  (pp. 113–114).
- **Time markers:** "Almost every man wore boots, up to the time of the Rebellion; now
  nearly all wear shoes" (p. 13); "I have visited Washington several times in later
  years" (p. 9); the dead — "some sleep in old Ocean's sepulcher… The *Sea Gull*'s crew…
  had sunk together" (p. 263).
- **The Conclusion (pp. 309–311):** "a line in the log-book is the only record of the
  peril we confronted"; "It is now sixty years since my dear mother gave me her parting
  blessing… I find I am nearing the end of life's voyage"; the Heavenly Flag Ship "under
  one loving Lord High Admiral… no respecter of persons"; **final printed line of the
  book: "Shipmates, shall we meet together there?"** [⚑ "sixty years" strains his
  chronology — sailor's round number; flag, don't smooth.]
- **Negative space (all NOT FOUND in the text):** pensions, reunions, G.A.R., lectures
  in the writing-present, his 1890s address, wife or children in the present, his exact
  age, **any account of the 1842 courts-martial or Wilkes' later career/death** beyond
  the title page's "late Admiral." The trial act must come from Reynolds, the press, and
  the record — the memoir is silent, and the silence is usable.

## Part III — The man in 1890 (web pass; verified vs. lead)

- **Life dates:** born Roxbury 1822 or 1823 (source-internal discrepancy), **died 1900**
  per Dartmouth's archival agent record — in 1890 he is ~67–68 with ten years to live.
  [VERIFY exact dates via MA vital records / Boston Globe obituary ⚑]
- **★★ THE PANORAMA IS DOCUMENTED:** Dartmouth Rauner holds "Erskine's Panorama Lecture:
  Around the World in Search of the Antarctic Continent Under Capt. Wilkes" (ms., 1859)
  — delivered at the Roxbury Mechanic's Institute beside a moving mural of Ex.Ex. scenes
  (finding aid: 11,000 sq ft; Boston press accounts in the collection). Finding-aid scene
  sequence: **Madeira, Rio, the Andes, St. Lorenzo Island, Tongataboo, the Antarctic
  Continent, a Fiji island, Cook's Bay in the Sandwich Islands.** **He packaged the
  voyage for a paying public in 1859; the 1890 memoir is his second telling — and the
  pin night was in neither until now.** [VERIFY details via Rauner scans ⚑]
- **Publication sequence:** 1890 self-published (Boston) → 1896 Jacobs trade edition
  (Philadelphia). **Any frame scene involving a publisher must be author-publication;
  Jacobs did not exist in 1890.**
- **Panorama-circuit economics** [W]: moving panoramas toured the US 1848–1860s
  (Purrington & Russell's whaling panorama the type specimen); ~25¢ admission, ~2-hour
  narrated showings — the model available to a common sailor.
- **Memoir weather of the 1880s–90s** [W]: Century's "Battles and Leaders" (1884–87),
  Grant's subscription-sold *Memoirs* (1885) — the market that made an old sailor's
  self-published book thinkable.
- **Later life, occupation, family, grave: NOT FOUND on the open web.** Designated
  lead-mine: Thomas Philbrick's introductions to the 1985 Smithsonian facsimile and 2006

## Part IV — What this settles and what it corrects

1. **The frame is real:** 1890 is the actual year of authorship-into-print; the desk,
   the self-publication, the "late Admiral" on the title page (Wilkes 13 years dead),
   the colt on the cover, the paired portraits — all [E]. GAP 2 is closed to
   first-draft standard; remaining wants are biographical (address, family, grave).
2. **⚑ CORRECTION (spine-grade): the flogging predates the expedition.** Erskine's own
   text (pp. 10–11): coast-survey *Porpoise*, "Captain Charles Wilkes commander,"
   Charlestown Navy Yard, fall of the year (~1837); Boyle gives the order at the
   gangway; Hampton Roads muster later confirms "it was Commodore Wilkes at whose
   command I had been flogged." The base's mid-Atlantic 1838 flogging and CLAUDE.md's
   spine line need `fact:` correction — **the mid-Atlantic night is the pin, not the
   lash.**
3. **⚑ CORRECTION: Wilkes is awake at the pin moment** — "sitting at a table tracing
   out a chart." CLAUDE.md's "sleeping commander" does not match the primary.
4. **The literacy arc, complete and dated** (the Signal's spine, all [E]): mark on the
   articles (~1835, "unable to write at the time," p. 7) → Rio, late 1838: Bill Roberts
   reads letters aloud; the ship's yeoman (never named) teaches him from "straight
   marks," then the word **"mother"** — "covered many fathoms of paper with that
   precious name" (p. 39) → Mar 1840: whole written vocabulary itemized — "mother,
   home, sisters, brothers, Roxbury, Boston, Big Dick, Torrente, Hurrah for Jackson,
   all nations!" (p. 129) → **Honolulu re-enlistment: "wrote my name in full, and felt
   as big and grand as though I were an officer"** — his first full signature, given to
   the expedition's articles (pp. 203–204) → "My First Letter," Honolulu 1841, printed
   in the book in facsimile spelling: "Mother, Mother, Dear Mother… Your absent son
   Charlie," with the $100 remittance via purser Waldron to F. D. Quincy, 25 Commercial
   St. (p. 204) → the Maine academy, two months, $1/week, late 1842 (pp. 272–273).
   **No teacher is ever named. The record hands the novel the arc and leaves every
   teacher anonymous — invention licensed, flagged.**
5. **The oath is asserted, never shown** (searched exhaustively): only "sworn to be
   revenged" (p. 16) and "I remembered my oath" (p. 19). The oath scene is a true gap —
   [I] licensed under the fact-gate, to be flagged in the author's note.
6. **The letters that got wet were the ship's official mail** — from the navy agent,
   including "the sailing orders, which the captain expected" (p. 11). Not the boy's
   own, not other sailors'. The flogging was over the commander's own paperwork,
   damp. [E]
7. **The mother:** given name NOT FOUND ("a Sturtevant, of Dutch descent"; brother Maj.
   Thomas Sturtevant of Roxbury); her scenes inventoried (blessing, the visit before
   the errand, the quarter-mile earshot, the pin-night face, the word "mother," the
   $100, the Canton Street reunion, "It was always mother, mother, mother with me").
   Death: not in the text.
8. **He went back to sea** after 1842: ~a decade of merchant voyages (incl. the clipper
   *Rainbow*, NY–Canton 1845, 7 months 17 days) ending with a Philadelphia–Boston brig
   run and marriage to Jennie (Father Streeter, North Bennett Street). "My seven years
   in the navy" (p. 203); rejected the navy for good after hearing the *Somers*
   hangings discussed. The Second Signing's afterlife is [E].
