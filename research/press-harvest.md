## Part I — Niles' National Register, the homecoming-to-verdict season (verified extracts)

### 1. The homecoming week — June 11, 1842 (v62 #1602)
Congress was already moving "a bill to provide for publishing an account of the discoveries
made by the exploring expedition, **under the command of Lieutenant Wilkes**" — the rank in
the legislative record from day one, and the squadron's return sharing column space with a
resolution on hemp cultivation. The national attention, measured exactly. → serves: ch. 31
"to nothing," now checkable in tone as well as fact.

### 2. Two weeks home, and the priority fight is already public — June 25, 1842 (v62 #1604)
- The Ross/Wilkes chart dispute litigated in print: Wilkes' "circular chart of the
  Antarctic ice… with the full explanatory letter which was furnished him by Captain
  Wilkes (previous to Captain R.'s antarctic cruise)," the "Bellamy 1839" land placement,
  and the note that Wilkes' report "published in 1840" had staked the claims. The chart
  gift [E-base ch. 24] and its consequences were newspaper matter within a fortnight of
  landfall.
- **★ Scene found:** a National Institution meeting in Washington, "at which were present
  Mr. Poinsett" — "Lieut. Wilkes gave a brief narration of his late voyage, **vindicated
  the American discoveries**…" The commander, still a lieutenant, defending the continent
  at a lectern before his trial, with the man who gave him the squadron in the audience.
  Dated ~June 20, 1842 [VERIFY exact date from the full item].
- Squadron texture: "the brigs Porpoise and Oregon, now on their way home… expected early
  in July" — the expedition came home piecemeal; the *Vincennes*' lonely June 10 was not
  even the whole fleet. → serves: homecoming chapter structure.

### 3. The trial machinery — July 23, 1842 (v62 #1608)
- The reciprocal structure, in one paragraph: trials of "Pinckney, lieutenant Johnson,
  assistant surgeon Gil[lou?], passed midshipman May, lieutenant comd't. Reynolds, captain
  Walker, and others, on recriminating charges. Lieut. commandant Wilkes will be tried, by
  the same court, on charges preferred by some of his late officers." ⚑ **[VERIFY the
  roster against the trial record — OCR garbles names; "lieutenant comd't. Reynolds" must
  be identified before any use** (if it is *our* Reynolds, that is a major story beat the
  base lacks; do not assume).]
- The court: "com's. Barron, Stewart, Biddle, Downes, Tatnall, and five lieutenants" ⚑
  [VERIFY — if **James Barron** (the man who killed Decatur) and **John Downes** sat in
  judgment, the bench itself is a chapter of Navy history glaring down at Wilkes].
- **★ "The court has been ordered, by the special request of lieut. Wilkes, to investigate
  the causes of some misunderstanding which arose between lieut. Wilkes and some of his
  officers"** (crediting the N.Y. Journal of Commerce). He asked for it — Braid 1's last
  self-inflicted wound, reported at the time.

### 4. The reprimand genre, demonstrated — August 13, 1842 (v62 #1611)
A complete Secretary-of-the-Navy public reprimand (to one of the officers Wilkes accused;
addressee to confirm): guilty of "disrespect to your superior in the execution of his
office… although it involves no moral turpitude, [it] strikes at the foundation of all
discipline. A respectful deportment is part of the duty of obedience, and obedience is the
first law of military service…" → serves: the exact bureaucratic liturgy Wilkes himself
will face; the trial act can quote the genre before it turns on him.

### 5. Acquittals and leaked orders — August 20, 1842 (v62 #1612)
- Verbatim acquittal letter: Lieut. **Robert E. Johnson** (the Cascades overland leader),
  tried "upon charges of 'disobedience of orders and **wasting the public property**'…
  preferred by lieut. Charles Wilkes, acquitted… — A. P. Upshur." What Wilkes prosecuted
  his officers *for*, in the government's own words.
- The press printing "extract[s] from **private instructions issued to lieut. Wilkes by
  James K. Paulding… dated [1]4th December, 1839**" — the confidential orders becoming
  public evidence mid-trial. The paper trail turning on its keeper. → serves: F2's
  documents-as-fate theme, sourced.

### 6. The verdict sealed — September 10, 1842 (v63 #1615)
"The defence of lieut. Wilkes was read before the Naval Court Martial at New York, on
Tuesday. At the conclusion of the defence, the Court remained in secret session for some
time making up its verdict, which when decided upon was transmitted to the Navy Department
at Washington." Same issue: the publication act became law — "under the command of
**lieutenant** Wilkes" — alongside "An act to regulate the pay of pursers." → serves: the
verdict-in-transit beat; the statute's rank.

### 7. ⚑ NEW EVENT — the marines' civil suits — September 17, 1842 (v63 #1616)
"Four marines, who allege themselves to have been maltreated by Lieut. Wilkes, on board of
the Vincennes, and other vessels of the exploring expedition, have brought several suits
against that officer, in the U.S. Court, New York, and laid their damages to a large
amount." **Absent from the base entirely.** Enlisted men suing their commander *as
citizens*, in civil court, in parallel with the courts-martial — the Upshur reprimand's
"rights of others of her citizens" line has a live context. [VERIFY outcome of the suits —
follow in later Niles' issues / court records ⚑ high-value thread.]

### 8. ★★ THE SENTENCE, VERBATIM — October 1, 1842 (v63 #1618, running head "TRIAL OF LIEUT. WILKES")
> "Navy department, 22d September, 1842. — Sir: You have been duly tried by a court
> martial… and sentenced to [be publicly reprimanded by] the secretary of the navy, at
> such time and place as he may deem proper. This sentence is approved. **The country
> which honored you with a command far above the just claims of your rank in the navy,
> had a right to expect that you would, at least, pay a scrupulous respect to her laws.
> The rebuke, which, by the judgment and advice of your associates in the service, she
> now gives you, for having violated those laws in an important particular, involving
> the rights of others of her citizens, will be regarded by all as the mildest form in
> which she could express her displeasure.** I am respectfully your obedient servant,
> A. P. UPSHUR. — Lieut. Charles Wilkes."

Every clause lands on a nerve this book has been tracking: the rebuke delivered *through*
the rank wound ("far above the just claims of your rank"); the flogged men named as
citizens with rights; the closing shrug ("the mildest form"). The trial act's final
document, found whole. [OCR gap: the sentence's middle clause — "…and sentenced to [be
publicly reprimanded by]…" — is bridged from context; **verify the exact wording against
the scan before quoting in the base** ⚑.]
Same issue: trial exhibits reprinted — Wilkes' Sydney dispatch of March 11, 1840 "read by
the judge advocate on August 25th," and testimony touching the Antarctic instruments ("for
the dip, and our azimuth compass was so sluggish…") — the continent itself under
cross-examination. Court members noted: "Lieuts. James T. Gerry, and Samuel F. Dupont;
Judge advocate — Charles H. Winder" [VERIFY roles — Du Pont's later fame makes this worth
a line if confirmed].
