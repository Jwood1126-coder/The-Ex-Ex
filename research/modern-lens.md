## 1. Psychology

- **Charlie's wound is textbook betrayal trauma — T3 vocabulary, T2 mechanism.** Modern
  trauma research distinguishes harm from strangers from harm by attachment figures; the
  second produces exactly Charlie's profile: shame rather than grievance, concealment
  (fifty years of secret), intrusive imagery (the mother's face recurring in sea and sky),
  somatic memory ("even then felt the sting of the boatswain's colt" — his body, not his
  narrative, keeps the score), and hypervigilance around the perpetrator. **Never use the
  vocabulary; write the behaviors** — flinches at the bosun's call, positioning himself
  where Wilkes isn't, the body remembering before the mind does. His late-life arc
  (literacy, the panorama lectures, the memoir, the "Ship Universal" dedication) is what
  the research calls meaning-making and post-traumatic growth — T3 as words, T2 as shape:
  the confession *is* the therapy, which the novel can dramatize without naming.
- **The pin moment through an adolescent brain — T2.** Charlie was fifteen–sixteen:
  impulse-regulation circuitry a decade from mature, threat-arousal at maximum. Modern
  developmental science says the shocking thing is not that a flogged sixteen-year-old
  held the pin — it's that he put it back. His lifelong self-condemnation ("a criminal in
  thought") judges a boy's midnight by a grown deacon's daylight. The 1890 frame can hold
  this compassion *as an old man's earned wisdom* without one anachronistic word.
- **Wilkes reads as a narcissistically wounded overachiever — T3 diagnosis, T2 dynamics.**
  Grandiosity riding on fragility; insatiable for the one blessing (Bowditch) and
  hypersensitive to its withholding (the salute, "Mister Wilkes"); rank-costume as
  self-repair; rage at mirrors (the officers he'd made). Modern power research adds a
  mechanism: authority measurably erodes empathy and inflates self-attribution — command
  *itself* fed the spiral. And chronic sleep deprivation (he worked his charts at all
  hours — [E], the pin scene found him at it past midnight) degrades exactly the
  judgment and emotional regulation he most lacked. **Hard rule 11 stands: the lens
  explains, it never simplifies or excuses, and none of its language touches the page.**
- **The ship was a total institution — T2.** Modern sociology of closed institutions
  (ships, prisons, barracks) predicts the Ex. Ex.'s pathologies precisely: rumor as the
  information economy of the powerless (the fo'c'sle telegraph), surveillance breeding
  performance (the spyglass), grievance compounding at interest in a system with no exit.
  There *was* an exit ledger: desertion at every green port — the institution's own
  pressure gauge.
- **Malolo's mechanics are crowd-violence science — T2, handle under the ledger rules.**
  Ambiguous signal + armed fear + no shared language = threat-perception cascade; modern
  research on massacre adds moral disengagement's four levers — euphemism ("chastisement"),
  diffusion of responsibility (orders, volleys), dehumanization, and grief-fueled
  aggression (documented: bereaved men are disinhibited toward violence framed as
  justice). Wilkes' "total and terrifying" grief converting to two burned towns is a
  studied human pathway, not an aberration — which is the double ledger's point made
  clinical. **T3 absolute on vocabulary; the base's Río-Negro-machinery framing is
  already the correct period-voiced rendering of the same science.**
- **The ice cruise is a polar-psychology case study — T2, T1 possible.** Modern
  winter-over research (Antarctic stations, submarines) documents the syndrome the
  Vincennes lived: monotony + confinement + no true night = irritability, insomnia,
  cognitive fog, factionalism. Their sixty days below the sixties in perpetual daylight,
  never standing down, is the modern literature's stressor list verbatim. T1 candidate:
  a wonder-aside noting that men now paid to winter in Antarctica, with heated stations
  and psychologists, still break along exactly these seams [VERIFY phrasing against
  current literature before use].

## 2. Medicine

- **Scurvy's cruelest trick — T2, T1-grade image.** Vitamin C builds collagen; without it
  the body stops maintaining connective tissue — which is why advanced scurvy *reopens
  healed wounds and old scars*. The age knew the fact without the mechanism (Anson's
  voyage recorded old wounds opening); we know why. The Ex. Ex. largely outran scurvy
  with fresh provisioning [E-consistent], so apply as *dread*, not event — the men knew
  what long salt-ration legs could do: the sea could literally unwrite your healings.
  (The metaphor writes itself onto Charlie's back; use only if the record ever puts
  depletion aboard — otherwise it stays the period's ambient fear. [VERIFY squadron
  health record in Wilkes' appendices.])
- **Cold physiology — T2 everywhere in Acts II/IV.** Cold-shock gasping in the first
  minute of immersion; swimming failure in ten to thirty; why Stewart's boots buying
  "just long enough" is medically exact. Wet wool insulates (~half its dry value); wet
  cotton kills. Alcohol *feels* warming (vasodilation) while accelerating heat loss —
  the grog they craved in the ice was a quiet enemy. "Sound enough to haul, too worn to
  warm" is chronic cold exposure plus caloric deficit: hauling in polar conditions burns
  5,000+ kcal/day against a salt ration nowhere near it [VERIFY numbers before T1 use].
- **Sleep science under watch-and-watch — T2.** Chronic four-hour fragmentation produces
  impairment comparable to intoxication (reaction time, judgment, mood). Every fall from
  aloft, every snapped temper, every misread signal in this story happened inside that
  deficit — and the Antarctic's no-night stripped even the body's circadian anchor. The
  surgeons' Act IV letter ("the crew was breaking") describes, in 1840 language, a
  condition modern fatigue science can graph.
- **The medicine chest was often the enemy — T2.** Calomel (mercury) for most ailments —
  salivation, tooth loss, tremor were *treatment* effects; bleeding for fevers weakened
  the fevered. A sailor avoiding the sick bay was often making a rational calculation.
  Ether anesthesia arrived in 1846 — every surgical scene in this book is speed, rum,
  leather, and held-down screaming, by law of the calendar.
- **Snow blindness is sunburned corneas — T1 already half-planted.** Photokeratitis: UV
  scorching the corneal surface; agonizing, temporary, preventable with slit-goggles the
  Inuit had and the Navy didn't. The Mauna Loa porters' snow blindness [E] and the ice
  cruise's glare share one mechanism the period couldn't name.

## 3. Ocean, weather, ice, earth

- **Why the Horn is the worst place — T1-grade.** The Drake Passage is the only latitude
  band on Earth where the ocean circles the planet uninterrupted by land: infinite fetch,
  waves building without limit, the Antarctic Circumpolar Current funneled through a
  600-mile gap. The sailors' dread has a map-shaped cause. → the base's ch. 13 essay can
  take one sentence of this across time.
- **The Antarctic Convergence is real physics — T1, already used.** The base's "invisible
  frontier where the water abruptly chills and the whole ecosystem changes managers" is
  the Antarctic Polar Front — a density wall where cold surface water dives under warmer;
  modern oceanography confirmed their thermometers were crossing an actual boundary, one
  of Earth's largest, invisible everywhere but in the data. Their towed thermometers [E]
  were doing modern science early.
- **The gales that nearly killed the *Vincennes* have a name now: katabatic winds — T1
  ★★.** The East Antarctic ice sheet drains cold, dense air downslope by gravity;
  along the Adélie/Wilkes coast it arrives at the sea as sudden hurricane-force rivers of
  air out of clear weather. The region they charted was later measured as **the windiest
  sea-level place on Earth** (Cape Denison, "home of the blizzard," a few hundred miles
  from their track [VERIFY distance]). Piner's Bay's instant gale wasn't weather's malice
  — it was the continent itself, exhaling. *The thing that almost killed them was the
  strongest evidence the land was real.* That is a wonder-aside of the first order.
- **Ice-blink and water-sky are optics — T2/T1.** Pack ice reflects white onto cloud
  bases; open leads reflect dark: the sky was a live chart of ice beyond the horizon,
  and the sailors read it correctly without knowing it was albedo. Tabular bergs = ice-
  *shelf* calvings: the "ice islands" they quarried for drinking water [E] were pieces of
  a continent's floating apron — they drank the land before they proved it.
- **Looming has physics — T1, serves the trial.** Cold dense air over polar seas bends
  light downward (superior mirage/refraction), lofting real coasts into view across
  impossible distances — and modern reassessment of Wilkes' plotted coast shows much of
  his "error" was *real land displaced by refraction*, not invention [VERIFY the specific
  modern reconstructions before T1 use]. Science eventually testified for the defendant,
  a century late.
- **The Columbia bar — T2.** The largest collision of river discharge and wave energy on
  the U.S. coast; the bar rebuilds itself with every freshet, which is *why* the sailing
  directions were stale and why the survey existed. The *Peacock* died of sediment
  transport.
- **Tarawa's "faithless tides" now have harmonics — T1 ★, closes the book's longest
  echo.** The November 20, 1943 assault landed on an apogean neap tide — a periodic
  low-water regime Tarawa's lagoon is now known for; modern tidal analysis explains
  exactly the blank the 1841 boats never stayed to fill. The base's final pages can say,
  across time: we can compute today the tide that failed them; the computation is the
  monument to the sounding never taken. [VERIFY tidal specifics against the USNI/
  literature before wording.]
- **Darwin and Dana were right, and the proof took an atomic age — T1 ★.** The
  subsidence theory of atolls was confirmed in 1952 when drills at Enewetak passed
  through ~1,200+ meters of coral and struck volcanic basement [VERIFY figure] — the
  drowned mountain exactly where Darwin said it must be, found by crews preparing
  thermonuclear tests at an atoll charted in the Ex. Ex.'s ocean. The wonder-aside
  pairs with the sounding-lead passage in Act III: the answer beneath their lead was
  reached, eventually, by a drill.
- **Rogue waves were real all along — T2.** Sailors' "mountains of water" were long
  discounted as yarns; instrumental confirmation (Draupner, 1995) vindicated the
  fo'c'sle's memory. License to trust the witnesses' seas.
- **The auroras they kept logging ride the solar cycle — T2/T1.** Solar cycle 8 peaked
  ~1837; the voyage sailed down its bright slope — their repeated southern-lights nights
  [Erskine map] were the sun's weather, on schedule. [VERIFY cycle dates before T1 use.]
- **A research question worth one lookup:** their freakishly mild February 1839 Horn
  passage ("mild, clear, smooth" [E]) — does it sit on an El Niño year in the paleo-ENSO
  reconstructions? If yes, the sea's one act of mercy has a climate signature. [OPEN —
  check reconstruction literature; assert nothing until checked.]
- **Deep geography they sailed without knowing — T1 sparingly.** The "shattered" Fuegian
  coast is a plate boundary's wreckage; Mauna Loa sits on the hotspot that made the whole
  island chain in sequence; the Pacific's floor — whose depth the base compares to
  airliners — was unmappable to them beyond the sounding line's reach. One aside per
  hundred pages, maximum; the geology must never outtalk the deck.

## 4. History and historiography (what later knowledge does to the story)

- **The Pacific they charted was demographically collapsing — T2, ledger-grade.**
  Introduced disease had been emptying Polynesia since Cook; Hawaiʻi's population by the
  1840s was a fraction of contact-era estimates (figures contested [VERIFY range]). The
  "closing blank spaces" theme has a grimmer stratum: the blankness was partly made —
  emptied — by the contact the charts served. Use under the double ledger's tilt tests.
- **Modern Pacific historiography pushes back on pure-victim narratives — T2, matches
  the book's own rules.** Current scholarship emphasizes Islander agency: Fiji's wars as
  Fijian politics (Bau vs. Rewa, Cakobau's rise) into which the squadron blundered as a
  piece on someone else's board — exactly the base's framing. The witnesses' dignity rule
  is, happily, where the field went.
- **Fijian memory of Malolo exists — OPEN, dignity-critical.** The base already carries
  the old man's "some eighty." Whether fuller oral-historical or Fijian-scholarly accounts
  of the 1840 attack exist is a real research question for a later session's web pass —
  the one archive the archive test most wants. [OPEN]
- **The gold under their boots — T1 ★.** The Emmons overland party marched down the
  Sacramento Valley and provisioned at Sutter's fort in 1841 — seven years before the
  strike at Sutter's mill remade the continent they'd surveyed. The Ex. Ex. walked across
  the gold rush without knowing it, and its California charts waited at the harbor's
  mouth for the world that came.
- **Their data still works — T1.** Ships' magnetic and meteorological logs of this era
  feed modern reconstructions of Earth's field and climate [VERIFY whether Ex. Ex. logs
  specifically are ingested]; the Tarawa chart's 1943 afterlife [E] is only the loudest
  case of a general truth: the expedition's measurements outlived every man who took
  them, and some are still being consulted by sciences that didn't exist when the
  pendulums swung.
