> **Why these two.** Both are public-domain, book-length eyewitness accounts by expedition

## 1. George M. Colvocoresses, *Four Years in a Government Exploring Expedition* (1852/1855)

- **Local text:** `colvocoresses.txt` (636 KB; Archive.org `Fouryearsgovern00Colv` /
  `fouryearsingove01colvgoog`). Identity-verified (Wilkes ×65).
- **Who he was:** midshipman (1816–1872), Greek-born — himself a survivor of the Chios
  massacre, ransomed as a boy and raised in Vermont [VERIFY details] — serving aboard the
  *Vincennes*; a member of the **Emmons overland party** (Columbia → Sacramento, Sept–Oct
  1841), which makes him a primary eyewitness for the one campaign where Erskine and
  Reynolds both go thin.
- **Expected yield:** the overland march (base act_5 ch. 30 gives it two sentences);
  officer-deck texture of the middle acts; port calls; a second count on contested events.
  His own biography (massacre survivor charting the Pacific for the country that adopted
  him) is coda-grade if the record supports a line [VERIFY].
- **Status:** acquired; harvest not started.

## 2. Joseph G. Clark, *Lights and Shadows of Sailor Life* (1848)

- **Local text:** `clark.txt` (617 KB; Archive.org `lightsshadowsofs00clar` family).
  Identity-verified (flogging/Vincennes hits present).
- **Who he was:** a sailor of the *Vincennes* — **a second forecastle voice beside
  Charlie's**, and written in 1848, forty-two years closer to the events than Erskine's
  1890 memory. Where Clark and Erskine agree, the below-decks record is corroborated;
  where they differ, Clark's recency arbitrates [VERIFY his exact rating/berth from the
  text or muster].
- **Expected yield:** forecastle dailiness (grounding families A–D); independent witness on
  the floggings, the ice cruise, and (his title promises "thrilling incidents") the
  Fiji events; religious-tract framing to discount (the genre's piety is its tax).
- **Status:** acquired; harvest not started.
