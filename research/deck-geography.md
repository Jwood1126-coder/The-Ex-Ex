## 1. The hull — dimensions, tonnage, crew

- **Length between perpendiculars 127 ft; beam 33 ft 9 in to 34.75 ft** (sources
  conflict — Wikipedia/DANFS lineage vs. Smithsonian usexex site; state as range); depth
  of hold/draft ~16.5 ft. Two independent modern references agree on 127 ft.
  **VERIFIED** — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Vincennes_(1826);
  sil.si.edu/DigitalCollections/usexex/html/vincennes.htm
- **Tonnage 700–780 — state the range, do not clean up.** Modern references: 700–703
  tons. Wilkes himself calls her "seven hundred and eighty tons" (*Narrative* I, p. xv).
  **VERIFIED** (conflict honestly reported).
- **Crew ~190–200.** SIL usexex gives the ExEx crew as 190; Erskine (aboard her): "the
  crew was so large — two hundred," divided into **sixteen messes of twelve**, watches
  starboard/larboard, stations: forecastle, fore/main/mizzen tops, waist, afterguard.
  Charlie was a **mizzen-topman** ("one of the forties… the never-sweats"). TYBTM
  pp. 17–18. **VERIFIED** (primary + secondary agree).
- **Landsman's scale:** a hair longer than a basketball court plus a school bus, about
  as wide as a two-lane road, carrying ~190–200 men.

## 2. The refit — CORRECTION: light spar deck, NOT a poop cabin

**The `[verify]` on "she received an added poop cabin/roundhouse for the cruise" is
FALSE for the *Vincennes*.** Wilkes, *Narrative* I, p. xv:

> "The Vincennes was a sloop of war, of seven hundred and eighty tons, originally
> single-decked, but in consequence of the intended cruise, a light deck was put on her
> for the protection of the men, and to afford more room. The accommodations thus became
> those of a small frigate."

The poop-cabin-and-forecastle alteration Wilkes describes on the next page is the
**Porpoise's** — the brig Charlie sailed to Norfolk in (*Narrative* I, p. xvi: "This was
to build a poop-cabin and a forecastle on her deck"). Wikipedia dates the *Vincennes*'
light-spar-deck refit to her 1836 decommissioning/remodeling — i.e., after the
expedition was authorized (May 1836). **VERIFIED ⚑** — corrects the tasking's [verify];
poop cabin/roundhouse claim for *Vincennes* = NOT FOUND.

## 3. The deck stack

Three decks, named by the pin scene's own witness (TYBTM p. 17): **spar deck** (weather,
with quarterdeck aft), **gun deck** (covered, guns — the Fijian king was astonished at
"the number and size of our guns" on his tour of "all the decks," Colvocoresses
pp. 141–142), **berth deck** (crew berthing; Colvocoresses also uses "berth-deck").
The added light deck is why she shows a frigate-like double row of ports in the
Disappointment Bay engraving. **VERIFIED** (two primaries).

**Vertical zoning, in Wilkes' own order** (Aug 31, 1839, *Narrative* I Appendix,
p. 433 — PD, quotable): "Hereafter no specimens of coral, live shells, or any thing
else that may produce a bad smell, will be taken below the spar-deck, or into any of
the rooms" — the crew's world was vertically zoned, and the gun deck stank of drying
coral until this order. **VERIFIED**.

## 4. The quarterdeck

- **Period definition** (Dana, *Seaman's Friend*, 1847 dictionary): "QUARTER-DECK. That
  part of the upper deck abaft the main-mast"; the waist lies between quarterdeck and
  forecastle. On the modified *Vincennes* the quarterdeck is the after stretch of the
  new spar deck: mizzen mast, wheel, binnacle, cabin skylight, companionway, taffrail at
  the stern rail, the two after corners being the quarters. **Definition VERIFIED;
  Vincennes-specific fitting order is [W]-convention.**
- **Wheel and binnacle:** wheel with "the tiller-ropes leading up to it, and secured
  around its barrel" (Totten); binnacle "placed near the helm," quartermaster attending
  "the helm and binnacle at sea" (Dana); a dog-vane of feathers "placed on the weather
  side of the quarter-deck, for the direction of the helmsman and officers" (Totten,
  p. 439). The Fijian king marveled at the wheel (Colvocoresses pp. 141–142). Sister-ship
  *Peacock*'s wheel-ropes ran to the rudder head (carried away by ice, Jan 24, 1840,
  *Narrative* II). **Exact position of the *Vincennes*' wheel relative to the mizzen:
  UNKNOWN** — period US convention **[W]** puts it just abaft the mizzen, forward of the
  cabin skylight.
- **Companionways:** "Companion-ladder is that by which officers ascend to, or descend
  from, the quarter-deck" (Totten). An after companion connecting quarterdeck to
  cabin/gun deck is standard **[W]**; its exact position on *Vincennes* **UNKNOWN** (no
  inboard plan found).
- **Lookout stations at night:** in vessels of war lookouts "are placed in many parts
  forward and aft"; the OOD's hail "Keep a good look-out" is answered "Ay, ay, sir!" or
  by calling their stations, as "Starboard cat-head" (Totten, s.v. LOOK-OUT). Erskine
  confirms a **standing lee-quarter lookout** on the *Vincennes* (TYBTM p. 19). Typical
  set: cat-heads/bows forward, gangways, quarters aft. **[W]-convention, quotable; exact
  *Vincennes* station bill UNKNOWN.**

## 5. The pin scene's verified geometry (crown jewel)

Erskine, midnight Aug 29–30, 1838 (TYBTM pp. 19–20). **VERIFIED primary** (single
witness — flag remains per corpus self-audit):

- His station: **lookout on the lee quarter** — the after corner of the quarterdeck,
  downwind side.
- From there he was "looking down the cabin skylight" at Wilkes "sitting at a table
  tracing out a chart" **one deck below** — so the skylight sits on the quarterdeck
  centerline abaft the wheel, within a step or two of the lee-quarter station, directly
  over the after cabin's table.
- He took an **iron** belaying pin "from the rack" when "the officer of the deck walked
  forward":
  > "I watched my opportunity, and as the officer of the deck walked forward I grabbed
  > an iron belaying-pin from the rack. In an instant it was suspended over the
  > commodore's head, while I paused a moment, waiting for the ship's weather roll."
- He held it over the open skylight "waiting for the ship's weather roll" — the roll
  toward the weather side, which would carry the drop across to the lee-set table.

**The OOD weather-side convention — the scene's clock, period-correct.** Dana (1847),
watch duty: "In a man-of-war there is always a lieutenant of the watch on the weather
side of the quarter-deck, whatever work may be going forward, except in the single case
of all hands being called to work ship." The weather side belongs to captain/OOD;
subordinates keep to lee. So Charlie at the LEE quarter is across the deck from the
OOD's beat; when the OOD "walked forward" (toward the waist/mainmast), the after lee
corner fell out of every eye except the helmsman's — who faces forward over his
binnacle. **VERIFIED convention + primary.**

**Night sightlines ([W]-inference from verified elements):** the only lights aloft of
the cabin were the binnacle lamp (helmsman's face) and the glow up through the cabin
skylight (Wilkes' lamp below) — Erskine could see the chart under Wilkes' hands from
deck level, which requires the skylight open or its glass clear and lit from within.
"Silence reigned supreme" except "a slight roll." Moon: not stated for the 29th
(moonrise noted the 22nd). **Skylight open vs. glazed: UNKNOWN.** Treat lamp/binnacle
staging as [W]-inference, not fact.

**How the boy got under the skylight (VERIFIED, TYBTM pp. 15–16):** Aug 17, 1838,
Hampton Roads — "the commodore's gig came alongside, bringing orders for me with my bag
and hammock"; Ringgold "must obey orders"; Charlie was rowed to the *Vincennes* as the
squadron weighed — twelve days before the pin. Wilkes ordered the boy who had sworn
revenge into his own ship. ("It seemed to me that I should sink through the deck. I felt
more like jumping overboard than sailing with my worst enemy, and one on whom I had
sworn to be revenged.")

## 6. Pin-rails, racks, and the pins themselves

- **Brady (USN, 1841, item 63, pp. 54–55):** "A Belaying Pin Rack… a piece of wood which
  has a number of holes through it, in which belaying pins are stuck; on the back part
  are several scores for the shroud to lie in, to which it is seized" — i.e., **racks
  seized to the shrouds**. Mizzen shrouds come down at the quarters, so a shroud rack at
  the mizzen rigging is the natural "rack" within reach of the lee-quarter lookout; a
  mizzen fife-rail with pins is standard **[W]**. NOTE: Brady's **tops carry
  belaying-CLEATS, not pin racks** ("Placing belaying-cleats in Tops"). **VERIFIED
  manual definitions ⚑; which rack Erskine used UNKNOWN** (shroud rack most consistent).
- **Iron belaying pins aboard — twice attested** (same witness, two contexts; ships
  differ): "I grabbed an iron belaying-pin from the rack" (*Vincennes*, 1838, TYBTM
  pp. 19–20); and at Fiji the *Peacock*'s crew's donkey skit — "Iron belaying-pins were
  put into their hands, which served for feet… strike the deck with the iron pins"
  (TYBTM pp. 160–161). Iron pins were ordinary issue in the squadron, heavy enough to
  ring on a deck. **VERIFIED.**
- **Pin dimensions/spec: UNKNOWN — report honestly.** NOT FOUND in any period source
  examined (Brady 1841/1852 tables, Totten, Dana); no USN dimension spec located. Do not
  state a length in the base; if the novel needs heft, render sensation (weight, cold
  iron, hand-filling girth) without numbers.

## 7. The commander's cabin below

Verified interior facts only: Wilkes worked seated at a table tracing charts at midnight
(Erskine pp. 19–20); the cabin was large enough to host the Fijian king's council and a
collation for a large company, "the sight of so many new things as the cabin presented"
exciting wonder (Colvocoresses pp. 141–142); magnetic dip was observed "in the cabin" as
distinct from "the ward-room" (Wilkes *Narrative* II, ~p. 322 area). The oft-repeated
detail set "stateroom and pantry, large reception room with drafting tables and chart
library" surfaced only in an unattributed search summary — treat as **PLAUSIBLE
secondary** (likely Stanton/Viola), NOT verified. Stern windows/quarter galleries for

## 8. ⚑ Base contradiction — act_1.md (pin geometry)
