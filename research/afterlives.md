## Part I — William Reynolds (1815–1879)

Primary secondary anchor: the Franklin & Marshall College Reynolds Family Papers
LibGuide (the repository of the family papers) —
https://library.fandm.edu/c.php?g=940538&p=7942102 (Significant Biographical Dates) and
https://library.fandm.edu/c.php?g=940538&p=6778574 (Biographical Timeline).

- **Health leave and the Honolulu years — VERIFIED.** Resided in Kauai and Honolulu,
  Hawaii, 1852–1861, during a leave of absence; formally placed on the reserved list for
  ill health **24 Sep 1855**; appointed **Naval Store Keeper at Honolulu, 23 Feb 1857**.
  Corroborated by Wikipedia and a Garden Island (Kauai) local-history piece
  (https://www.thegardenisland.com/2023/09/09/lifestyles/island-history-naval-officer-william-reynolds-resided-at-malumalu-from-1852-61/).
- **Return to service and ranks — VERIFIED with one discrepancy.** Appointed **Commander
  9 Jun 1862** (command of storeship USS Vermont, South Atlantic Blockading Squadron,
  Port Royal SC — an earlier fetch's "USS Tennessee for 1862" is an extraction artifact;
  Vermont is correct, Tennessee was his 1875 flagship); **Captain** — F&M gives
  commission date 25 Jul 1866, Wikipedia says promoted 1865 ⚑ [VERIFY against a Navy
  Register or his Johnson-signed commission; commissions often postdate the effective
  rank]; **Commodore 1870**; **Rear Admiral 12 Dec 1873** (solid via F&M). Commanded the
  **Asiatic Station** from flagship USS Tennessee, 1875. **Retired 10 Dec 1877** for
  failing health. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Reynolds_(naval_officer)
- **Acting Secretary of the Navy — CONFIRMED at year level.** Served as Acting Secretary
  of the Navy on two occasions, **1873 and 1874**, during George M. Robeson's tenure
  under Grant (two independent secondary sources agree; Wikipedia says "in 1874"). Exact
  dates of the two stints NOT FOUND — would be confirmed in the 1873–74 Annual Reports of
  the Secretary of the Navy or contemporary Army and Navy Journal issues. **Do not assert
  specific months without that check.** The corpus assertion holds.
- **Midway, 28 August 1867 — VERIFIED.** As captain commanding USS Lackawanna, Reynolds
  took formal possession of Midway Atoll (then Brooks' Island) for the United States,
  under orders of the Secretary of the Navy — **the first offshore island annexation by
  the US** (Lackawanna had reached Honolulu 9 Feb 1867). Date solid across
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Lackawanna_(1862) and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Atoll; the NHHC "today in history" page lists it
  but fetch failed (cert error) — retry for a .mil citation. His own report (US
  government document, PD): "thus concerned in taking possession of the first island
  ever added to the dominion of the United States beyond our shores" — quoted at
  https://www.eaglespeak.us/2007/08/midway-island-claimed-for-us-in-1867.html?m=1;
  ⚑ [VERIFY wording against the original report (SecNav Annual Report 1867 or House Ex.
  Doc.) before printing]. The pen that recorded the Ex. Ex. ends by annexing an island
  the expedition's own method mapped.
- **Death — VERIFIED.** Died **5 November 1879** in Washington, D.C.; buried next to his
  brother in Lancaster, Pennsylvania.
- **Brother John Fulton Reynolds — VERIFIED.** Major General John F. Reynolds (b. 20 Sep
  1820, d. 1 Jul 1863), killed on the first day of Gettysburg, was William's YOUNGER
  brother (William b. 1815 is the elder). Wikipedia ("John F. Reynolds"): "Two of his
  brothers were James LeFevre Reynolds, Quartermaster General of Pennsylvania, and Rear
  Admiral William Reynolds." The brothers are buried side by side in Lancaster.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Reynolds

## Part II — Charles Wilkes (1798–1877)

Best single PD backbone for the 1890 frame: the 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica entry
(Wikisource, transcription verified against page-scan wikitext) —
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Wilkes,_Charles
(Page:EB1911 - Volume 28.djvu/662).

- **1842 court-martial — VERIFIED (sentence detail [VERIFY]).** On return (New York,
  June 10, 1842) Wilkes was court-martialled and, per EB1911 (PD, quotable): "He was
  court-martialled on his return, but was acquitted on all charges except that of
  illegally punishing men in his squadron." "Public reprimand" as the sentence comes
  from secondary summaries — ⚑ confirm in Stanton or the court-martial record.
- **Narrative publication — state the discrepancy.** Wikipedia: published 1844 in 5
  volumes plus atlas; EB1911 lists editions "6 vols., 1845; 5 vols., 1850." Cite "1844,
  5 vols. and atlas" for the first edition, noting EB1911's differing edition dates.
- **1844–1861, the reports — VERIFIED.** Chiefly engaged preparing the expedition's
  official reports: **28 volumes planned, only 19 published (1844–1874)**; he himself
  wrote the Narrative, Hydrography and Meteorology (1851), *Western America* (1849), and
  *Theory of the Winds* (1856); briefly attached to the Coast Survey. Promoted commander
  1843, captain 1855.
- **Trent Affair — VERIFIED.** Commanding USS San Jacinto, on **November 8, 1861** he
  stopped the British mail packet Trent with two shots across her bow and removed
  Confederate commissioners James M. Mason and John Slidell; officially thanked by
  Congress, briefly a national hero; the action disavowed by Lincoln and the
  commissioners released to avert war with Britain. (Exact release date, late Dec
  1861–Jan 1862, not verified this pass — check before using.)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Wilkes
- **Civil War commands after Trent — UNCERTAIN on exact dates.** James River Flotilla
  (1862), then the West India (Flying) Squadron against blockade runners, holding
  commodore rank and styled acting rear admiral. EB1911 says he was "disrated (becoming
  a captain on the retired list)" as too old for the commodore promotion, feeding his
  feud with Secretary Gideon Welles — but EB1911's internal dates self-contradict
  (commodore 16 July 1863 vs disrated November 1862); other sources put the commodore
  commission in July 1862. ⚑ Use "commodore, 1862; rank later revoked in the Welles
  feud" unless a Navy Register is checked.
- **1864 court-martial — VERIFIED (medium-high on exact date).** Welles criticized
  Wilkes severely in his December 1863 annual report; Wilkes' scathing reply reached the
  newspapers. Court-martialled and on **April 26, 1864** found guilty of disobedience of
  orders, insubordination and other specifications; sentenced to **public reprimand and
  three years' suspension; Lincoln reduced the suspension to one year**, after which
  Wilkes retired. Sources: NHHC DANFS "Wilkes II" (page itself cert-errored; facts via
  search summary —
  https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/w/wilkes-ii.html);
  EB1911 (guilty verdict, 3-year sentence). Original court record digitized at Gilder
  Lehrman GLC00267.348 (https://www.gilderlehrman.org/collection/glc00267348) if primary
  confirmation wanted.
- **Rear admiral, retired list — VERIFIED.** EB1911: "on the 25th of July 1866 he was
  promoted to the rank of rear-admiral on the retired list" — the rank an 1890 memoirist
  would know him by ("Admiral Wilkes").
- **Death — VERIFIED.** "He died at Washington on the 8th of February 1877" (EB1911),
  aged 78. Buried at **Oak Hill Cemetery, Georgetown**; funeral reportedly at St. John's
  Episcopal Church, Lafayette Square (⚑ St. John's detail from search summaries only —
  confirm against a Feb 1877 Washington paper).
- **1877 obituaries — tone NOT FOUND (verbatim).** Chronicling America was
  Cloudflare-blocked from the research environment; NYT 1877 paywalled. One lead: the
  Yorkville (S.C.) Enquirer of Feb 15, 1877 carried a death notice (via a Geni page that
  would not render). Secondary scholarship (Stanton 1975; Philbrick 2003 — leads only,
  no words) holds that Wilkes died in relative obscurity, the expedition eclipsed in
  public memory by the Civil War and the Trent notoriety. ⚑ Re-run from an unblocked
  connection: "Admiral Wilkes," Feb 9–16, 1877.
- **1909 Arlington reinterment — year VERIFIED, details NOT FOUND.** Originally buried
  at Oak Hill; in **August 1909** the remains were moved to Arlington National Cemetery
  (per Wikipedia; sponsor/ceremony details unconfirmed — Find a Grave and
  arlingtoncemetery.net blocked). **Frame note: in 1890 he still lay at Oak Hill — an
  1890 narrator cannot know of Arlington.**
- **Autobiography — VERIFIED.** Written in longhand in **1871**, from diaries and
  memory; unpublished for a century: *Autobiography of Rear Admiral Charles Wilkes,
  U.S. Navy, 1798–1877*, ed. Morgan, Tyler, Leonhart, Loughlin (Washington: Naval
  History Division, 1978, xxii + 944 pp.). **Frame note: in 1890 the autobiography is
  unknown.** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Autobiography_of_Rear_Admiral_Charles_Wilkes ;
  https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000032079
- **The Antarctic claim in 1890 — VERIFIED as EB1911's claim.** "That Wilkes discovered
  an Antarctic continent was long doubted, and one of the charges against him when he
  was court-martialled was that he had fabricated this discovery" (EB1911 footnote, PD).
  Vindication came only after 1890 — EB1911 credits Shackleton's 1908–09 expedition with
  corroborating Wilkes, and Wilkes Land was named in his honour. **So in 1890 an old
  sailor knows the discovery as still contested** (Ross had disputed it in the 1840s).
- **What Erskine himself (1890) called him — VERIFIED.** The memoir's prefatory
  acknowledgment (PD, archive.org `twentyyearsbefor00ersk_0`, OCR ~310–316): "I have
  refreshed my memory by reading the history of our cruise in the united states
  exploring expedition, under the command of **the late Admiral Charles Wilkes**, and
  wish to acknowledge my indebtedness to the same for dates and a few facts and
  illustrations." The 1890 frame's narrator writes thirteen years after Wilkes' death,
  uses the retired-list rank, and names him **without rancor** in the acknowledgment
  while attacking flogging in the body ("Fifty years have passed, and how little reform
  has been made in the treatment of sailors!" — p. 264, OCR ~10688–10693) — a usable
  tension.

## Frame texture summary (1890)

Wilkes is 13 years dead, buried at Oak Hill (not Arlington), his Antarctic discovery
still disputed, his autobiography unknown; Reynolds is 11 years dead, a rear admiral
buried beside his Gettysburg brother in Lancaster, the man who annexed America's first
offshore island; and Erskine calls his old commander "the late Admiral Charles Wilkes"
without rancor while indicting the system that flogged him.
