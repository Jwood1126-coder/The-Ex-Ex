## Part 0 — The single biggest problem

**The dual-witness engine runs on one cylinder for the middle of the book — and at the
geographic climax it runs on none.** The engine is the book's stated design: Charlie the body,
Reynolds the pen, Wilkes the weather both live under. Map witness *presence* (embodied, not
merely mentioned) against the voyage and the hole is exact:

| act | Charlie (body) | Reynolds (pen) | what the engine misses |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | rich — flogging, Bowditch, the pin | present — Depot, letters to Lydia | nothing; the engine is set here |
| II | present — the tree, the slave ship, Sugarloaf | dominant — journal open, launch, *Sea Gull* gale | nothing; the model act |
| III | **zero mentions** | alone — suspension, Tuetila, fête | the unraveling's middle steps, single-witnessed |
| IV | 2 positional mentions, no senses | masthead Jan 16, then *gone* — the *Peacock* retires Jan 24 | the entire February traverse |
| V | returns as *meaning* (the verdict rhyme), not as body | thin — 3 mentions | Fiji and Honolulu from below decks |

Two consequences, one per witness:

- **Reynolds' exit is structural, not optional.** He transferred to the *Peacock* for the ice
  campaign [E — act_4, ch. 21], and the *Peacock* left the ice on January 24. From then to
  Termination Land — Piner Bay, the officers overruled, the February 12 avowal, the whole
  spine of the continent — the book's pen is a thousand miles away. The base papers over this
  with Wilkes' own account ("the best writing he ever did"), which works for a reference and
  cannot work for the novel: Wilkes is never a POV.
- **Charlie was there the whole time, and the camera never turns on.** He was before the mast
  of the *Vincennes* through the entire ice cruise [E — act_4, ch. 21] — present for Piner
  Bay, for the frozen rigging, for the sick list the surgeons put on paper. He is also aboard
  the flagship through every Act III port. The body-witness is *on stage and unused* for
  roughly forty percent of the voyage. This is the same finding as pass 1's grounding
  diagnosis, seen from above: the prose has no body because the book's designated body has no
  scenes.

**Why this is the biggest problem:** every other structural issue in this file — proportion,
the sagging third quarter, the frame — is either caused by it or cured through it. The ice
epic reads compressed *because* no witness carries it. Act III reads thin *because* one
witness carries it alone. The frame feels inert *because* old Erskine has nothing to remember
in the middle acts — his young self isn't in them.

**Fix directions** (compatible, not exclusive):
1. ★ **Assign the February traverse to Charlie now, in the chapter map's assumptions,** and
   let the Erskine read-through confirm the material. His memoir spans the voyage; what TYBTM
   gives the ice campaign is the single highest-stakes unknown in the source base. **The
   read-through therefore carries an architecture question, not just fact-checks:** does
   Erskine give Acts III–IV enough body to sustain alternation? Everything in `08-chapter-map`
   waits on that answer.
2. **Alternate by movement, not by strict chapter rotation** — let Reynolds own the Atlantic
   and the Peacock-in-the-ice; let Charlie own the February traverse, Honolulu (the gratings
   are his category of wound), and the trial's below-decks view. Strict alternation would
   starve on the real distribution of sources; movement-scale alternation matches it.
3. **[BASE], cheap:** the generic-sailor camera (02, step 1) can restore the forecastle's
   presence in Acts III–IV without any new event-facts — the base's fix is grounding, not
   restructuring.

## Part I — The spine audit: one unraveling, three braids

The master prompt asks whether the slight-chain (*Independence* salute → Tuetila → Sydney fête
→ Malolo) is a clear escalating spine. Verdict: **the spine is present and nearly complete in
the base — but it is three braided chains, not one, and the four-beat chain as stated
conflates them.** Naming the braids matters because they escalate by different mechanisms and
the novel must wire them differently:

**Braid 1 — slights received (the fuel).** The promotion promised and withheld (act_1, ch. 6)
→ the salute affair and "Mister Wilkes" (act_2, ch. 10) → the captain's uniform and broad
pennant, self-awarded (act_3, ch. 17) → the Sydney fête refusal, "not dependent upon them"
(act_3, ch. 20) → the pennant hauled down "himself, in the harbor, unwatched" (act_5, ch. 30)
→ the courts-martial (ch. 31). This braid is *about rank* and runs on humiliation in;
its terminal image is already perfect.

**Braid 2 — the wound passed downward (the transmission).** Wilkes does to the powerless what
the Navy does to him: Charlie flogged for an accident (act_1, ch. 5) → the spyglass and the
squalls (act_2, ch. 10) → the *Relief* as demonstration, "a way home, and the commander held
the manifest" (act_3, ch. 17) → Tuetila snubbed — the identical wound he suffered at Rio,
dealt to a chief who walked mountains for him (act_3, ch. 19) → Reynolds suspended over five
minutes (ch. 19) → the officers' written opinions overruled in the ice (act_4, ch. 23) → the
Honolulu gratings past twelve (act_5, ch. 28). This braid is *about power* and runs on
humiliation out.

**Braid 3 — the machinery of mutual fear (the escalator).** No villain required: Río Negro,
two frightened parties arming against each other's defenses, cost: one evening (act_2, ch. 12)
→ Reao, bird-shot, "the first shots" (act_3, ch. 18) → the Opotuno manhunt, writ-as-far-as-
the-guns (ch. 19) → the hostage-machinery at Malolo, cost: 74–104 dead and two towns (act_5,
ch. 27) → Upolu bombarded, Utiroa burned over a man never found (ch. 29). This braid is
*about contact* and runs on standing procedure plus fear.

**The junction is Malolo, and the base has already wired it** — it only needs to be *seen* as
the junction: Braid 2's messenger (Underwood, who carried the Tuetila snub to Wilkes' door and
was marked by the narrative for it) and Braid 1's one soft spot (Wilkes Henry, the nephew, the
promotion-world's only pure gift) both die inside Braid 3's machinery — and the grief of the
man at the center converts all three braids into a massacre. That is the architecture of the
moral climax, and it is *causal*, not thematic. The novel's job is to make each rung visibly
ignite the next; the base (correctly, as reference) narrates the rungs locally and leaves the
wire slack.

**The ice is not an interruption of the spine — it is its Act IV rung.** The overruled
surgeons' report is Braid 2 at its most productive: "the officers were right about the men,
and Wilkes was right about the coast, and he spent the one to get the other." Same appetite,
aimed at glory instead of grievance. Played this way, the reader never gets to file Wilkes
under villain during his greatest month — which is rule 11 achieved by structure instead of
by assertion.

**Missing rungs / base enrich candidates** (post-read-through, `enrich:` commits):
- Braid 1 lacks one beat between Sydney (Dec 1839) and the homecoming (1842) — the rank
  grievance goes quiet for two years. Candidate: any documented mid-voyage flare (the
  acting-appointment revocations, if the record dates them) [VERIFY — read-throughs].
- Braid 2's Honolulu rung would carry more if one flogged man is namable [VERIFY — debt #4,
  02 §VI.4].
- Braid 3 plants beautifully at Río Negro; consider one sentence there acknowledging the
  *procedure* (hostage-surety as standard practice) so Malolo's machinery is recognized, not
  learned, on arrival. The base already does this for the fear-arithmetic; the hostage
  convention currently first appears at Vendovi (act_5, ch. 26), *after* it's needed.

## Part II — Proportion: where the book must slow down

The base's own numbers, against what each act must become in the novel:

| act | base words | content | novel pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 5,330 | ten years of politics + the making | **compress** — politics become texture |
| II | 7,841 | Atlantic, Horn, first ice, the launch | hold — the model act, richest sources |
| III | 4,822 | eight months, five ports, the turn | **expand** — the unraveling's middle |
| IV | 4,422 | *the continent* | **expand ~3×** — currently the shortest act |
| V | 6,378 | Fiji → wreck → home → trial → codas | **split** — it is two acts wearing one coat |

Three findings:

1. **Act IV is the shortest act in the book and it contains Antarctica.** The single most
   disproportionate fact in the manuscript. As reference, defensible; as blueprint, the novel
   must grow the ice from ~4,400 words of summary into the book's physical core — and Part 0
   says with whose body.

2. **Act V is two acts.** Fiji-through-wreck (the moral climax and its consequences) and
   homecoming-through-codas (the reckoning) are different movements with different engines —
   the first is Braid 3 detonating, the second is Braid 1 and 2 coming home to court. The
   novel should treat them as separate acts (making six, or five with Act I folded into the
   Atlantic act — see Part VI, decision 4). The trial currently gets **one paragraph** (ch.
   31) for a season of reciprocal courts-martial in which the surgeon Guillou led the evidence
   [E] and the paper the voyage generated — required journals, demanded correspondence, the
   written opinions Wilkes collected in the ice "a lawyer's instinct that would serve everyone
   at the courts-martial" [E] — came back as ammunition [VERIFY the mechanics during
   read-throughs]. For a book whose witnesses are *documents*, the trial is not aftermath; it
   is the payoff of the book's central conceit, and the novel should spend a real act-part
   there.

3. **The third-quarter repetition problem: Utiroa lands on Malolo's bruise.** In the base,
   ch. 29 runs the pattern again — bombardment, landing, a town burned, "as volleys had broken
   Malolo's" — at lower intensity, eight months later, over a man never found. Risk in the
   novel: anticlimax-by-repetition; the reader has met the darkest thing and this reads as
   its echo. Options:
   - (a) Compress Utiroa to aftermath-texture inside a Honolulu-to-Columbia passage.
   - (b) ★ **Reframe it as the darker beat it actually is: the machine now runs without
     grief.** Malolo had a devastated uncle at its center; Utiroa has *procedure* — no rage,
     no nephew, a missing muster entry, and a town burns anyway. Same acts, colder engine —
     that is escalation of meaning at lower volume, which is exactly what a third quarter can
     carry. One page, deliberate, flat-voiced; the flatness is the point.
   - (c) Keep parallel weight with Malolo — honest but structurally expensive; the ledger's
     catalog (06) can hold the parity even if the page-count doesn't.
   The wreck of the *Peacock* then works as designed — the false-relief beat where the sea
   collects the ship instead of the men, and the castaway squadron is fed by the people it
   came to survey. And the homecoming's "to nothing" is the anticlimax *weaponized* — keep it
   flat; the trial supplies the third act's actual voltage.

## Part III — The frame: is 1890 scaffolding or architecture?

Current plan of record: old Erskine opens at the taffrail with the pin; the confession closes.
**As designed, the frame is bookends — used twice, inert between.** The middle acts give old
Erskine nothing to do and (per Part 0) his young self nothing to carry. Menu:

**F0 — Bookends (current plan).** Cheapest; proven shape. Cost: the frame contributes nothing
for 80% of the book, and the wonder-aside — the voice's signature — has no in-fiction anchor
(who is answering across time?).

**F1 — Frame returns at act joints.** Old Erskine at his desk, 5–6 brief interchapters, each
timed to re-aim the act it precedes (before Fiji: the old man circling the chapter he doesn't
want to write). Benefit: the frame earns its keep; the confession is *prepared*. Cost:
interchapter machinery can read as pacing tax if the returns don't each do new work.

**F2 ★ — The two documents ARE the frame.** The novel's organizing conceit: it is built from
an illegal journal and an impossible memoir. Reynolds' chapters ride the secret journal —
dated, present-tense-adjacent, written *under threat of discovery* (the Sept 13 order that
forbids it is already in the base [E, act_2 ch. 8]; the risk is dramatizable within the
record's silence). Charlie's chapters ride the 1890 memoir — retrospective, an old man
writing toward the one page he has never said aloud, his *ability to write at all* being the
voyage's longest arc (the illiterate boy who taught himself letters [E]). The two documents
converge on the trial, where the voyage's paper — required journals, written opinions —
becomes evidence [VERIFY mechanics], and the book's last motion is the memoir's dedication:
"To the Crew of the Ship Universal." Benefits: dual-POV is *justified*, not just deployed;
the wonder-aside gets its in-fiction anchor (old Erskine answering across time); the frame
recurs without interchapter furniture because it *is* the narration's fabric; secrecy gives
Reynolds' sections a standing tension no sea-peril has to supply. Costs: two temporal
registers to manage; risk of preciousness if the device is announced rather than inhabited;
Reynolds' journal-dates discipline constrains scene placement to attested moments (also a
provenance feature); requires the read-throughs to confirm both documents' coverage before
the chapter map locks.

**F3 — No frame.** Pin as 1838 cold open, straight chronology, 1890 epilogue only. Cheapest
honest alternative; loses the confession as *destination* and makes the codas do frame-work
they're not shaped for.

**The pin's double exposure (whichever frame wins).** The pin currently exists twice in
plan: the novel's 1890 opening *and* its chronological moment (eleven days out — mid-Atlantic,
which is Act II territory; the base fronts it as ch. 1 by flash-forward). The novel must
choose a distribution. ★ Recommended: three-beat split — the 1890 prologue shows the old man
and the pin *and stops before the choice*; the chronological scene, arriving in the Atlantic
act, completes the night in full; the 1890 close supplies the confession's words ("God
forgive me…"). One scene, three payments, no repetition — the frame's whole case made by its
handling of a single object. (Architecture only; the scene itself is crown-jewel territory
and stays Jake's — exemplar policy, decision log 2026-07-07.)

## Part IV — The preface: essay-open vs cold-open

**[BASE]: keep the essay preface.** Orientation is a reference document's job, and the
preface's core idea — *wonder is the passenger, never the purchaser* — is load-bearing for
the whole ledger. One flag, menu not verdict: the Tyson attribution. A 21st-century celebrity
scientist is the preface's one tonal foreign body in a book that otherwise speaks from inside
its period, and the framework (gods/kings, fear, profit) is the argument's furniture, not its
source. Options: (a) keep as is — honest about where the idea came from; (b) ★ keep the
three-patron ledger, cut the personality — state it as the book's own accounting ("the
ledgers of exploration know three patrons…") with Tyson credited in the back-matter note
where the style guide already sends the debts; (c) re-source the idea period-inward (Adams'
diary, the whale-oil arithmetic) — more work, purer, loses the clean triad. [NOVEL]: no essay
preface at all; the novel opens cold (the pin, per Part III) and the three-patron idea, if it
appears, belongs in old Erskine's mouth as hard-won crew wisdom, not as citation.

## Part V — Big swings (quarantined)

Radical reconceptions, argued honestly, none enacted. Guardrails: the five non-negotiables
only.

**BS-1 — The Trial Frame.** Recast the entire novel inside the 1842 courts-martial: acts as
charges, voyage as testimony, verdict as climax. *Benefit:* built-in engine; the reciprocal
prosecutions are inherently dramatic; documents-as-witnesses becomes literal. *Cost:*
courtroom-flashback is the genre's most worn chassis; it subordinates the Pacific to the
courtroom — the wonder, the ice, the encountered worlds all arrive pre-litigated; and the
actual verdict (acquittals plus one reprimand) is a legal anticlimax that cannot carry a
whole book's suspense. **Verdict: reject the frame; steal the act.** The trial becomes the
final act's spine (Part II.2), dramatized in full, with the journals-as-evidence beat as its
centerpiece [VERIFY mechanics].

**BS-2 — The Two Documents (F2, promoted to the book's conception).** Not just a frame
choice: the novel understood as the story of *how its own sources came to exist* — the
forbidden journal and the impossible memoir, the two acts of writing that preserved the
voyage's truth against the commander's monopoly on the record (he held the official journals;
he superintended the *Narrative*; the counter-record survived in a locked sea chest and an
old man's late-learned letters). *Benefit:* unifies POV, frame, theme, and the project's own
provenance ethic — the book becomes an argument for exactly the discipline this repo
practices; every structural element pays twice. *Cost:* as in Part III/F2 — register
management, preciousness risk, read-through-gated. **★ My recommendation, pending the
Erskine coverage answer.**

**BS-3 — The Third Witness: Vendovi as recurring presence.** Promote Vendovi from ch. 26's
(brilliant) set piece to a structural constant of the voyage's back half: the prisoner-chief
crossing the decks at intervals — Honolulu, the Columbia, the long way home — the Pacific
riding *inside* the expedition that came to chart it, watching America approach. His death
the day after landfall is already the voyage's epitaph in the base; structure would make it
land as an arrival the whole back half walked toward. *Benefit:* the ledger embodied in a
person rather than a rhetoric; the dignity rule executed as architecture. *Cost and
condition:* the record of his shipboard life is currently thin in this repo (debt #1, 02
§VI.4 — even which ship carried him needs confirming [VERIFY]); as POV he is untouchable
(interiority would be invention about a man the record flattened — dignity rule), so he
works as *observed constant* only, and only if the read-throughs supply real material.
**Verdict: adopt at "recurring presence" strength if and only if the sources feed it;
otherwise ch. 26 + the epitaph already honor him better than thin scenes would.**

**Considered and rejected — the Tarawa strobe.** Flash-forwards to November 1943 recurring
through the survey chapters (the chart being drawn / the chart being bled on). Rejected
because the 1943 payoff works *because it is held once*, at the end, as the ledger's last
entry; strobing it converts a devastating audit into a device. Logged so no one re-invents
it.
