## Part I — What the ledger is (and the two ledgers)

**A ledger records; it never nets.** The book's contract sentence — the chart in one hand,
the ledger in the other, "neither hand be allowed to open" — bans both tilts: toward
indictment (the voyage as crime with charts attached) and toward absolution (greatness with
regrettable costs). Both entries stay true at full strength in the same chapter. That is rule
6; this file is its craft manual.

The manuscript actually runs **two ledgers in different currencies**, and keeping them
distinct is the first discipline:

- **The moral ledger** — what the expedition *did*: Reao's bird-shot, the manhunts, Malolo,
  Utiroa, the gratings, the skull in the Patent Office. Its debits are inflicted.
- **The mortal ledger** — what the sea and the work *cost*: Porter's near-hanging, Stewart,
  the *Sea Gull*'s fifteen, the frostbitten ice crews, the *Peacock*'s bones. Its debits are
  paid.

**The no-cross-netting rule ★: entries in one ledger never offset entries in the other.**
American dead do not discount islander dead; hardship does not purchase absolution ("they
suffered too" is a tilt). The base already honors this instinctively — "No American died" at
Malolo is stated plainly, as its own line — and ch. 31's side-by-side accounting works
*because* both ledgers were kept separately for thirty chapters before being laid on one
page. Protect that instinct as law. (The act title "The Price of the Chart" flirts with
netting language; it survives as irony — the act itself shows who actually paid — but
in-line constructions like "the price worth paying" or "the cost of discovery" used as
absolution are banned. Price is a question in this book, never a settlement.)

The plant to protect: the word "ledger" enters the book at Rio — Charlie in the slave ship's
hold, the wardroom looking elsewhere, "What the century found remarkable, and what it didn't,
is its own kind of record, and this voyage keeps both ledgers" (act_2, ch. 11). The book's
moral instrument is calibrated on the slave trade *before* the Pacific ever tests it. First
entry, right position; never move it.

## Part III — The metronome diagnosis

Three repetitions, each distinct, each with its counter:

**1. The balanced-sentence template.** "X, and Y, both true" as a recurring shape — census
and budget live in `01-voice-bible.md` §II.3 (≤1 explicit two-hands sentence per act; the two
untouchables are Malolo's "It has to be written both ways…" and ch. 31's "both of those
clauses go on the stone").

**2. The closing-position habit.** The ledger beat repeatedly deployed as the chapter-ending
sting. Scan the act-V chapter ends: Fiji's "darkest chapter" tease, Vendovi's "timed by fate
like a verdict," Malolo's "neither hand be allowed to open," the wreck's "at least in the
record for the rest of us," the homecoming's skull paragraph. Five consecutive chapters
closing on the ledger teaches the reader the rhythm, and a taught rhythm is a dodged punch.
Counter: **vary the position** — mid-chapter ledger beats land harder for arriving
unannounced (the base's own best example is mid-paragraph: "No American died," sitting flat
in the middle of Malolo's account). At least one act-V chapter should end on something the
ledger can't audit — weather, work, a sail bent on.

**3. The narrator-umpire declaration.** "It has to be written both ways, because both are
true and each is a lie alone" — the narrator stepping to the podium to state the discipline.
Devastating exactly once. **Budget: once per book, and Malolo owns it.** Everywhere else the
ledger is *performed*, never announced: the reader who was told the rule at Malolo is trusted
to run it at Utiroa unprompted. (Cross-ref the directive test, 01 §II.1 — announcement is
the ledger's curation directive.)

**The grammar-level rule ★: "and" is the ledger's conjunction; "but" is a tilt.** "The charts
of Fiji the squadron carried off that August are magnificent — the boats went straight back
to surveying, and finished the group — … Malolo's dead have no stone." No "but" anywhere in
the sequence: subordinating either entry to the other nets the ledger inside the syntax
itself. Grep-able; enforce in every pass.

## Part IV — The variation matrix (the reusable discipline)

Seven delivery forms. The anti-repetition rule is rotation: **never the same form for two
consecutive ledger beats**, and every act uses at least three forms.

1. **Scene, then one dry line.** Default for embodied beats; the style guide's own
   preference. The event rendered, then a single flat sentence that refuses to conclude.
   (The ice campaign: the night of Piner Bay, then "the officers were right about the men,
   and Wilkes was right about the coast.")
2. **Asymmetry-with-silence.** When one side has no archive, the missing entry is rendered
   *as absence*, four words where forty would flatten: "Malolo's dead have no stone." The
   silence is the debit. Use wherever the record itself is the atrocity's accomplice.
3. **Plain figures.** The audit table, rhetoric withheld — ch. 31's full accounting is the
   licensed book-scale instance; small instances ("Thirty of three hundred") carry entire
   chapters. Numbers given room become screams; explained, they become statistics.
4. **The deferred debit.** Credit now, cost later, unannounced: the Tarawa chart inked in
   ch. 29, the tide arriving in ch. 31 — two years and two chapters apart, wired by one
   "remember"-free plant. Book-scale patience; the form that makes the reader the auditor.
5. **The embodied ledger.** A person carries both columns, no rhetoric required: Vendovi
   (justice and abduction in one body), Whippy (desertion and indispensability), the kanakas
   (the Pacific manning its own charting), Wilkes himself (the whole book's pair of hands).
   Cross-ref `04` Part III; this form is why the cast bible exists.
6. **The witnessed ledger.** A period voice states one column inside a framed quote; the
   narrator holds the other in his own sentence. Charlie on the slave brig is the model —
   the quote testifies, the frame audits ("this voyage keeps both ledgers").
7. **Structural juxtaposition.** Placement does everything; commentary abstains. Vendovi
   dies *the day after* landfall; the skull is exhibited *among* the wonders; the wrecked
   surveyors are fed *by the surveyed*. The calendar and the catalog are the book's driest
   ironists — let them speak uncaptioned.

**Form-to-beat guidance:** violence at scale → 2 or 7, never 1 at full sensorium (02
anti-pattern 9: ground the cost, not the killing). Accumulations → 3 or 4. People the record
kept → 5 or 6. The one declared instance → Malolo, already spent.

## Part V — The tilt tests

Run on any edit that touches a ledger beat; a failed test blocks the commit.

1. **The netting test.** Does any sentence subordinate one entry ("but," "worth," "price
   paid," "in fairness")? Rewrite with "and," or split the sentence.
2. **The cross-currency test.** Do American losses appear where they could read as offsets
   for inflicted dead (or vice versa)? Separate them by at least a paragraph and a form.
3. **The spectacle test.** Is atrocity acquiring sensory richness the voyage's ordinary
   scenes don't get? Ground the cost, not the killing (02, anti-pattern 9).
4. **The absolution test.** Period-standards framing ("not one government on Earth in 1840
   would have faulted the reprisal") must sit *beside* the act, never above it — the next
   sentence must return the full weight ("And: a United States exploring and scientific
   expedition… killed something near a hundred people"). The base's Malolo paragraph is the
   template; deviation from its shape needs an argument.
5. **The indictment test.** The achievement stays real: the charts are magnificent, Hale's
   map dignifies, Tarawa's reef lines held true. If an edit makes the credit column read as
   sarcasm, it tilted.
6. **The archive test ★.** Whose account is missing, and does the text say so? "An island
   whose side of July 24 exists in no archive, because the expedition burned the town and
   sailed away with the only pens" is the gold standard — the missing record is itself
   entered in the ledger. Every Class-3 beat (04, Part IV) gets an archive sentence or a
   reason it doesn't need one.

Consistency flag while auditing beat 18: the death toll appears as flat "twenty-eight" in the
preface and ch. 1 but as the honest range ("28 strict, nearer forty loose") in ch. 31. Rule 5
prefers the range where sources conflict; the early flat counts should either carry "by the
strictest count" or stay silent on the number. Filed in `11` [VERIFY].

## Part VI — [NOVEL] mechanics: the ledger without the narrator

In scene-mode the narrator-auditor is mostly unavailable — POV discipline means the ledger
must be carried by structure, objects, witnesses, and the frame:

- **Objects as ledger entries:** the pin, the stone with room left, the skull, the chart of
  Tarawa, Agate's portrait of Vendovi. Objects accumulate meaning across scenes without a
  word of commentary; the novel's ledger is substantially a props department.
- **The frame as auditor (if F2 wins):** old Erskine's 1890 hand can hold what young Charlie
  couldn't see — the confession chapters are where the moral ledger gets said aloud, in a
  character's voice instead of a narrator's, which is the only POV-legal home for beats the
  young witnesses missed.
- **Malolo as news ★ (recommended design, pending read-through).** Whether either witness
  stood on Malolo's beach is unconfirmed [VERIFY — Reynolds' and Erskine's whereabouts,
  July 24–26, 1840; read-through question]. If neither did, the constraint is the design:
  the novel's Malolo arrives as *news reaching the witnesses* — the smoke from the boats,
  the story passing gun to gun, the crawl described secondhand by men who can't stop
  describing it. Distance becomes dignity and dread at once, the spectacle problem never
  arises, and the reader receives the massacre the way the squadron did: as a thing already
  irreversible. If a witness *was* present, the scene stays cost-side only (form 1 + 2).
- **Utiroa in the novel** rides decision 5 in `05` Part VI — the ★ option (the machine
  without grief, played flat) is this file's form 7 executed at chapter scale.
