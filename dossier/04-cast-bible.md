## Part I — The engine

### Charlie Erskine — the body  ·  [BOTH]

**Arc (all [E], from the base):** fatherless Boston boy → chooses Wilkes as father on the
shoals survey → flogged for an accident, without a hearing, by the chosen father (the break)
→ signs on anyway, rated to the *Porpoise* to keep clear of the man → transferred to the
flagship ("The Navy would fix that soon enough") → the pin, eleven days out → four years
before the mast under the same pennant → twenty years at sea after → teaches himself his
letters → the panorama lectures → the 1890 memoir: the confession, the Smithson epigraph,
"To the Crew of the Ship Universal."

**Function.** The reader's proxy below decks; the book's pain-ledger; the counterweight that
keeps the charts from floating. Where Reynolds converts experience to judgment, Charlie
converts it to scar — the book's two currencies.

**Plants already wired (protect these):** the belaying pins racked in the top (ch. 1); the
mother — the vision that stays his hand, and "God bless you, my darling boy!" (chs. 1, 5);
Bowditch's fatherly advice, "be somebody" (ch. 4); the fresh scars under his shirt at sailing
(ch. 7); his illiteracy at signing — he cannot read the muster book that binds him (ch. 31
closes this: the man who taught himself letters wrote the book we're reading from).

**Returns that must land:** every lawful violence done to a body routes through his back —
the Honolulu gratings are *his category of wound* recurring at squadron scale; the 1842
verdict ("the only conviction it ever recorded against his captain was, in kind, *his*");
the codas. [NOVEL] note: Charlie's POV sound is sensory, concrete, feeling-first, analysis
never — the 1890 frame-voice (if F2 wins) is the same man plus rue and God.

**The gap (the book's known hole):** Acts III–IV, zero embodied use despite being aboard the
flagship throughout (05, Part 0). The Erskine read-through's architecture question: how much
body does TYBTM give Tahiti–Sydney and the ice? Everything about his novel-side load waits on
that answer.

### William Reynolds — the pen  ·  [BOTH]

**Arc (all [E]):** Depot acolyte — "thought the lieutenant a marvel and said so in letters to
his sister Lydia" → the two journals begin under the Sept 13 order (obedience and
insubordination in one act) → wonder apprenticeship: Rio, the Corcovado flag lark → the
launch survey, twenty-five days that set his prose on fire → the *Sea Gull* love letter →
the *Sea Gull* lost → the "bitter doses" → Tuetila ("Why, we could not tell!") → suspended
over five disputed minutes — the conversion ("the entries… start reading like a verdict
being drafted") → the fête entry: "The foul scamp — he should be hung" → transferred to the
*Peacock*, where "the tone of the quarterdeck let a man breathe" → the masthead, January 16
— sees the continent, recorded worst → the nip → Fiji boat duty → the commission that chased
him across the Pacific → Rebecca in nine weeks → the codas: Honolulu exile, Port Royal,
Midway, Acting Secretary of the Navy, Lancaster beside his brother.

**Function.** The instrument that converts admiration to hatred *with timestamps* — "plot the
two entries as fixes and the line between them is the truest track the expedition made." The
wardroom's eyes; the book's warmth; the proof that the record itself was an act of courage.

**Plants already wired:** Lydia as addressee — the journal is *to* someone, which is why it's
alive; codify for the novel: every Reynolds chapter is implicitly a letter to Lydia, that's
what keeps his voice correspondence rather than log. The Porter hanging meditation ("I always
thought hanging and drowning were easy deaths," ch. 8) sits early in a book where his ship's
dead drown unwitnessed (ch. 16) and his own pen later writes "he should be hung" (ch. 20) —
the base rightly never connects these aloud; the novel may seat them as quiet rhyme, once,
without comment. His "I had intended never to introduce such themes among these pages"
(ch. 18) is the pen fighting its own conversion — a plant for the journal-under-threat
tension if F2 wins.

**[VERIFY] flag:** the preface has Reynolds "twenty-two years old at the sailing"; act_1
ch. 7 has "twenty-three." One is wrong (birth date check — Franklin & Marshall timeline).
Filed in `11`.

### Charles Wilkes — the weather system  ·  [BOTH]

Never a POV. Read both ways in every scene, per hard rule 11. His witnessed arc is the spine
(05, Part I — the three braids); what belongs here is the *access discipline*:

**The three windows rule.** The record left exactly three honest channels into a man the book
must never enter, and the novel should confine itself to them:

1. **The names he put on the map** — the chart as his only candid diary: Disappointment Bay,
   Termination Land ("the chart speaking the commander's actual feelings"). Every Wilkes
   place-name is a mood entry with coordinates [E].
2. **His own prose when terrified** — "terror is the one subject that reliably burned the
   pomposity out of his prose" (ch. 23, Piner Bay). Quote him under fear; paraphrase him
   under vanity — fear is where his *Narrative* stops performing [E, quotes pending
   read-through page-cites].
3. **The witnessed body and its rituals** — pacing among the charts at all hours "as if the
   lines… were the only company he trusted" (ch. 1); the spyglass on his own ships (ch. 10);
   the uniform appearing unannounced (ch. 17); the pennant hauled down "himself, in the
   harbor, unwatched" (ch. 30). Ritual observed from outside, meaning supplied by the
   observer or by no one.

Anything outside the three windows — his thoughts, his griefs narrated from within — is [I]
about the one man the book has ruled off-limits, and it collapses the weather into a
character. The windows are how "never simplified" stays executable.

**The father-hunger triangle (novel-mode wiring, built entirely from [E]):** Bowditch's "the
neatest charts I have ever seen" is the one paternal blessing of Wilkes' life — "everything
he did afterward… can be read as a man trying to hear that sentence again" (ch. 4). Charlie
chose Wilkes as father and was flogged for an accident. Wilkes Henry is the widowed sister's
only son, the one place Wilkes' love had no rival — and Malolo kills him inside the machinery
Wilkes' own contempt helped arm. The triangle needs no invented interiority: the blessing,
the flogging, and the grief are all on the record, and the pattern states itself if the three
beats are kept visible to each other. (Bowditch died in 1838 [VERIFY exact date vs. the
charts presentation — if the blessing was among his last acts, note it and resist the urge to
underline it.)

## Part III — The supporting cast

Each entry: role → arc/recurrences already in the base → what the returns reveal → wants.

**Hudson** — the counter-Wilkes; proof the age permitted decency. Outranked Wilkes and served
under him anyway (the non-military classing, ch. 6); commands the *Peacock* through the nip
(ch. 23) and the wreck (ch. 30) — the two best-conducted disasters in the book — and gives
Reynolds a quarterdeck he can breathe on (ch. 21). His one flaw is *caution as vice*: he
declined to log the January 16 sighting on two young men's eyes, "and half the battles of
1842 would never have been fought" (chs. 22, 24). Function: same authority as Wilkes, no
wound — the control group that proves the tyranny was a choice. His returns should
accumulate quiet competence so the Jan 16 timidity lands as tragedy, not blame.

**Underwood** — the messenger who pays the message's bill. Carries the Tuetila snub to
Wilkes' door (ch. 19, planted: "worth a second look"); dies at Malolo inside the machinery of
the contempt he witnessed (ch. 27). The braid-junction made flesh (05, Part I). [NOVEL] want:
one or two embodied appearances between Samoa and Fiji so his death belongs to a person, not
a device — read-through: Underwood in Reynolds' boat-duty entries [VERIFY].

**Wilkes Henry** — the commander's one soft spot; the widowed sister's only son, his berth
personally arranged [E]. Function: Braid 1's hostage to fortune; the grief-charge Malolo
detonates. Currently appears only in death — the novel needs him *visible alive* at least
twice first (any documented shipboard presence: read-through want [VERIFY]).

**Ringgold** — the *Porpoise*; the service's median officer. "This is adventuring with
boldness!" among the bergs (ch. 14); pride-answers-pride in the French non-meeting (ch. 22).
Function: what ordinary looks like, so Wilkes and Hudson both read as departures. Thin in the
base; read-through may thicken [VERIFY].

**The scientifics (collective)** — "cargo that talked" (ch. 7); the permanent civil war of
shore vs. charts (ch. 9); furious inventory of Wilkes' instrument-shopping (ch. 4) planting
the corps war. Individually:
- **Dana** — the mind. Coral theory carried reef by reef toward Darwin's proof (ch. 18);
  Kilauea hands him "his life's other subject" (ch. 28). Wonder with rigor; the voyage as
  intellect. The one scientific whose arc has act-structure — use him when science must be
  felt as *quest*.
- **Hale** — the quiet genius; the counter-conquest. Reconstructing the peopling of the
  Pacific "from verb endings" (ch. 18) — knowledge without guns, and the in-book proof that
  the encountered peoples' own history is the deeper epic. Thematically load-bearing for the
  dignity rule; his work is the book's standing answer to the skull in the Patent Office.
- **Peale** (museum blood, crack shot, painter), **Pickering** (omnivorous), **Couthouy**
  (sea-captain conchologist — [VERIFY his documented collision with Wilkes; if confirmed,
  he is Braid 2 material and the scientifics' own Reynolds), **Drayton/Agate** — the eyes;
  Agate's Vendovi portrait is the one dignity the record gave the prisoner (plant it early
  if BS-3 proceeds).

**David Whippy** — the hinge. Nantucket deserter become "the indispensable hinge between two
worlds that could barely see each other" (ch. 25). Function: access — Fiji becomes legible

## Part IV — Dignity mechanics: the three witness-classes  ·  [BOTH]

Hard rule 7, operationalized. Every person the record flattened falls in one class, and each
class licenses different moves:

**Class 1 — Named, with record** (Vendovi, Whippy, Tuetila, Taufaʻāhau, George the pilot).
The record gives words, acts, or standing. License: scene presence, quoted/attested acts,
witnessed dignity. Ban: invented interiority; period slurs outside framed quotes.

**Class 2 — Named, without interior** (David Smith, John Sac, Jim Orange, the Fuegian
father). A name and a glimpse. License: keep them named, working, *specific* — presence
without ventriloquism; the narrator may note the record's flattening as fact (the base's own
move, ch. 19: "almost never allowed by its documents to be more than a phrase"). Ban:
promoting a glimpse to a personality; making them props for the witnesses' feelings.

**Class 3 — Collective, with agency** (the kanakas, the Hawaiian porters, the Clatsop and
Chinookan feeders of Peacockville, Malolo's dead). No individuals survive in the record.
License: collective action rendered as *action* — they man, carry, feed, refuse, submit,
survive; their competence and their costs stated plainly. Ban: scenery-fication; body-count
prose without the archive sentence ("exists in no archive, because the expedition burned the
town and sailed away with the only pens" is the gold standard — the absence itself is said
aloud).

Class assignments can move *up* on read-through evidence (a named kanaka, a Smith sentence in
Reynolds), never down.
